### I. Profile and Engagement
*(Space provided: 1-2 lines per question.)*

*   **Which profile are you engaging with:**
    Local Coaching Center Director / Subject Tutor (Tier 2/3 city).
*   **How well does this person fit the profile:**
    Perfect fit. Runs a 200-student coaching center and currently creates/grades mock tests manually.
*   **Type of engagement:**
    15-minute in-person interview and direct observation of their current test-grading workflow.

### II. Your General Script/Framework for Engagement 
*(Space provided: ~3 lines per item. Follows the Open-Ended ➔ Qualitative ➔ Quantitative flow.)*

*   **A. (Open-Ended):** "Can you walk me through your exact process for creating and grading a weekly mock test right now?" *(Goal: Identify their biggest time sinks).*
*   **B. (Qualitative):** "How do your students react when they transition from your paper tests to the official digital NTA exams?" *(Goal: Validate 'Simulated Reality Gap').*
*   **C. (Qualitative):** "What specifically prevents you from using existing digital tools like Moodle or Google Forms?" *(Goal: Uncover friction points).*
*   **D. (Quantitative):** "How many hours per week do you currently spend manually checking OMR sheets or test papers?"
*   **E. (Quantitative):** "How much would a platform that automates this process save you in monthly operating costs?"

### III. What did you learn?
Learned that teachers spend over 10 hours a week grading. They are terrified of losing top students to large EdTechs, but find existing LMS tools far too complex to set up.

### IV. What surprised you?
Surprised that the biggest friction wasn't software cost, but the time to *digitize* paper notes. Typing math equations/diagrams is a massive barrier for non-technical teachers.

### V. Which hypotheses did you seem to confirm? How and why?
Confirmed the "Simulated Reality Gap" is a huge selling point. The tutor mentioned parents complaining that students panic during digital JEE exams after practicing solely on paper.

### VI. Which hypotheses did you seem to invalidate? How and why?
Invalidated the idea that teachers want an AI to *generate* test questions. They dislike "generic" AI questions. They only want AI to digitize *their own* proprietary notes.

### VII. Which hypotheses were you unable to reach conclusions on? Why?
Unable to conclude the exact maximum price point. They complained about software costs, but couldn't pinpoint a budget since they view testing as a "sunk time" cost, not a cash expense.

### VIII. What new questions were raised in this engagement?
How can we build a "Zero-Code" PDF-to-test AI parser that requires absolutely no typing or formatting effort from the teacher?

### V. What changes should I make for the next engagement?

**Profile Changes:** *(Space: ~2 lines)*
Focus less on the "owner" and interview the "typist/DTP operator" or the junior subject teacher who actually does the manual data entry, as they feel the pain most acutely.

**Qualitative Insights/Hypotheses Updated:** *(Space: ~2 lines each)*
*   **A.** The "Simulated Reality Gap" is our strongest hook. We must lead with this emotional pain point instead of just "saving time."
*   **B.** Teachers will reject the software entirely if it requires them to learn LaTeX for math formulas. Our OCR parsing must be flawless.
*   **C.** Pricing should be pitched as a "cost per student" rather than a flat B2B SaaS fee to align better with their cash-flow models.

**Quantitative Insights/Hypotheses Updated:** *(Space: ~2 lines each)*
*   **A.** Test if saving them >10 hours/week translates to a willingness to pay ₹100/student/month.
*   **B.** Measure exactly how many PDF mock tests they generate per week (Hypothesis: >3 per batch).
*   **C.** Assess if an 80% reduction in grading time actually reduces their staffing/typist overhead costs.

**Script Update:** *(Space: ~3 lines each)*
*   **A.** *Change opening:* "I'm researching why students panic during digital CBT exams despite scoring well on your paper mock tests..."
*   **B.** Remove questions about "AI generating questions" as it alienates them and makes them feel easily replaceable.
*   **C.** *Add:* "If I could show you a way to convert your existing paper test into a CBT link in 60 seconds without typing, would you try it?"
*   **D.** *Add:* "Can you introduce me to the person who actually types or grades the papers in your center?"
