# Computer-Based Testing (CBT) Adoption in India: Ground Realities & Challenges

We talk a lot about digitizing education in India, but when it comes to conducting exams at scale, the transition to Computer-Based Testing (CBT) is far from simple.

Yes, flagship exams like JEE Main and CAT have proven that digital testing works. But expanding this model universally across the country is hitting some very real roadblocks.

Here is why scaling CBT in India is still such a massive hurdle:

1️⃣ The Infrastructure Bottleneck
Conducting a paperless exam for millions of candidates requires tens of thousands of standardized, secure computer centers simultaneously. Today, outside Tier-1 cities, those facilities simply don't exist in the required numbers.

2️⃣ The Rural-Urban Digital Divide
An online test assumes every student is comfortable using a computer. But for millions of test-takers from rural areas and smaller towns, basic digital familiarity is still a barrier. Testing should measure a student’s knowledge—not their comfort with a mouse and keyboard.

3️⃣ Sky-High Setup & Logistics Costs
Setting up a secure CBT venue isn't just about putting computers in a room. It requires uninterrupted power backups, localized server networks, surveillance systems, and dedicated technical staff across thousands of centers. The capital expenditure is huge.

4️⃣ Security & Technical Dependencies
When you shift from paper to software, your threat model changes. Server glitches, network downtime, software vendor vulnerabilities, and data breach risks can disrupt an exam for thousands of students in seconds.

5️⃣ Massive Candidate Volume
Managing multi-shift exams over several days for millions of applicants creates complex normalization challenges and puts enormous strain on existing digital infrastructure.

Moving to CBT isn't just about switching from paper to screens—it requires building robust, equitable infrastructure from the ground up.

Until we bridge the rural infrastructure gap, hybrid models and localized tech solutions will play a crucial role in making assessment truly fair and accessible for everyone.
