# How Indian coaching educators run and read mock tests today

Research for "Understanding Results" (2026-10-27). Sources: web research (linked below) and
TestoZa's own usage data (`TestoZa/Why_Teachers_Dont_Circulate_Tests_Oct2026_v2.md`).

## The weekly ritual

- **The unit is an occasion, not a test.** Saturday/Sunday weekly test, unit test, monthly test, full
  mock before the exam. Government-exam coaching (SSC, banking, railways) runs sectional and full
  mocks every week on OMR sheets "to simulate real exam conditions" (Resonance, IACE).
- **Checking is manual for most.** Large institutes scan OMR sheets with software. The small
  coaching centre or school teacher checks 40–200 copies by hand with a red pen, writes marks into a
  **register by roll number**, then makes a rank list. Indian rules hint at the load: Punjab's board
  expects 25 answer sheets per teacher per day, and Delhi government teachers spend about 50 hours a
  year (class teachers about 310) on exam work.
- **Results travel as rank lists.** Rank lists go up on a notice board, get photographed into the
  batch WhatsApp group, or are typed into Excel. Parents get marks at a PTM or on a call.
- **"Analysis" usually stops at marks and rank.** Question-wise analysis means tallying every answer of
  every student by hand (48 students × 20 questions = 960 ticks), so it rarely happens. The next-day
  "test discussion" class solves the whole paper from Q1, not the 4–5 questions the batch actually got
  wrong. Toppers' guides say real improvement comes from analysing mistakes (UPSC prep: 2.5–3.5 hours
  of analysis for a 2-hour paper), but that burden falls on the student, not the teacher.
- **Rules are tightening.** The Education Ministry's coaching guidelines (Jan 2024) say a coaching
  centre "shall not make public the result of assessment test conducted by it" and that tests should
  track performance and flag students who need counselling. So the video doesn't push public rank
  lists. It says "you choose who gets it" and focuses on private follow-up (call the student, message
  the parent).

## What this means for the educator watching

| Their worry when moving online | What the video shows |
|---|---|
| "Will I lose my register?" | Excel download: name, roll no, marks, rank, right/wrong/blank, time. |
| "Can I still give a rank list?" | Rank switch with real tie-breakers; Send rank list (WhatsApp), Copy, Print. |
| "Can I still open a student's copy?" | Every answer sheet: answers, section scores, topics, seconds per question. |
| "What about absentees / who left?" | Started / Writing now / Submitted / Dropped, with when they left and how much they had answered. |
| "My discussion class takes the whole period" | Teach these again: only the questions fewer than half got right, hardest first. |
| "Which chapter do I re-teach?" | Most-picked wrong option (trap), the left-blank share, even spread = guessing, section %. |
| "Is my batch improving?" | All Submissions: class average week by week. |

## TestoZa's own data (Oct 2026)

- 73–83% of Indian class submissions arrive 4 pm to midnight: TestoZa wins the **home-written** weekly
  test and DPP; the in-class paper exam is still on paper.
- Educators who run a first real exam come back (10 of 17 conductors ran a second exam), so the
  results screen is where TestoZa earns the next exam.
